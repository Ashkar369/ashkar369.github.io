The Northern Lights

Nature's light show in the night sky

The Northern Lights, also called the aurora borealis, are waves of coloured light that move across the sky near the Arctic. They appear when tiny charged particles sent out by the Sun reach the Earth. Our planet's magnetic field guides these particles towards the North and South Poles, where they crash into gases high up in the atmosphere and make them glow. Near the South Pole the same thing happens, and there it is called the aurora australis, or the Southern Lights.

The colour of the lights depends on which gas is hit and how high it is. Oxygen gives the most common green colour at around 100 to 200 km above the ground, and a rarer red glow even higher up. Nitrogen adds shades of blue and purple. The best places to see the aurora are northern countries such as Norway, Finland, Sweden and Iceland, on dark, clear nights in winter. When the Sun is very active, the lights can sometimes be seen much further south, even here in the Baltic countries.

Tags used on this page

  • <!DOCTYPE html> – tells the browser that this is an HTML5 document.
  • <html> – the main tag that holds the whole page.
  • <head> – holds information about the page that is not shown on screen.
  • <meta> – gives extra details to the browser, here the UTF-8 character set.
  • <title> – the name of the page shown on the browser tab.
  • <body> – holds all the content you can see on the page.
  • <h1> – the biggest and most important heading.
  • <h2> – a smaller heading used to start a new section.
  • <p> – a paragraph of normal text.
  • <br> – breaks the line so the text continues on the next line.
  • <ul> – a list with bullet points where order does not matter.
  • <ol> – a numbered list where order matters (type="a" and type="i" change the numbering).
  • <li> – one item inside a list.
  • <hr> – draws a horizontal line to separate parts of the page.
  • <div> – a box that groups content, here used with its own background and text colour.
  • <img> – shows a picture; src is the image address, alt describes it and width sets its size.
  • <a> – a link to another page; target="_blank" opens it in a new tab.
  • <!-- --> – a comment that helps the writer but is not shown on the page.
